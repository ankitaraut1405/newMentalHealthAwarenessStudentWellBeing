Relax & reset

Short exercises that may help many people feel calmer. Do as much or as little as you like. If anything feels uncomfortable, stop and breathe normally.

Breathing trainer

Ready
Sit comfortably. Press start when you are ready.

Why it helps: slow, even breaths with a longer breath out can ease the feeling of tension for many people.Use it when: before an exam or presentation, after a stressful message, or before bed.

Which exercise for which moment?

Before an exam

Try: Box breathing for 2 minutes, then one small first step.

Can't sleep

Try: Relaxing breath 4-7-8 for 5 minutes, then muscle relaxation. Screens off first.

After a fight or bad news

Try: Calm breathing 4-6 for 2 minutes, then a short walk.

Long study session

Try: The stretch break every 25 to 50 minutes.

Overthinking

Try: Calm sounds and the memory game to give your mind a rest.

Tense body

Try: Progressive muscle relaxation, one group at a time.

Progressive muscle relaxation

Why it helps: tensing then releasing muscles helps you notice and let go of tension.Use it when: your shoulders, jaw or hands feel tight, or at bedtime. Skip any area that hurts.

Calm sound

A soft, steady noise, like distant rain, to mask distractions.

Why it helps: steady sound can cover sudden noises that pull your attention.Use it when: studying, winding down or in a noisy hostel. Keep the volume low.

5-minute reset

  • Minute 1: pause, drop your shoulders
  • Minute 2: breathe gently
  • Minute 3: notice what is around you
  • Minute 4: stretch or move
  • Minute 5: choose one small next step
Use it when: you feel stuck or switching between tasks.

Study-break stretch

  • Roll your shoulders back 5 times
  • Gently turn your neck side to side
  • Stand, reach up, then reach toward your toes
  • Look away from the screen for 20 seconds
Use it when: after every 25 to 50 minutes of study. Stop if anything hurts.
