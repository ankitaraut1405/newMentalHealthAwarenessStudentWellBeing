Healthy habits, kept simple

Food

Balanced plates, regular meals, water through the day, and simple snacks like fruit or nuts on exam days. Check allergies and your own food preferences. If you have a medical condition, ask your doctor or a dietitian.

Movement

A 5 to 15 minute walk or stretch is a good start. Comfortable movement you enjoy beats a strict plan.

Sleep

A regular bedtime, less screen time late at night and a short wind-down routine can help. In our survey, 84% of students slept under 7 hours.

If you notice skipping meals, extreme dieting or feeling guilty about eating, please talk to someone you trust or a professional. See Get Support.

Starting a hard conversation

You do not need a perfect speech. The goal is simply to help the other person understand what is going on and what kind of support would feel useful.

1. Choose a comfortable moment

Pick a place and time where you can talk without rushing. You can also start with a message if speaking face-to-face feels difficult.

2. Start with “I”

Try: “I have been feeling stressed lately,” or “I have something I want to talk about.” Speaking about your own experience can make the conversation easier to begin.

3. Say what you need

You can ask for listening, practical help, study support, company, or help finding a trusted adult or professional.

4. If they do not understand

Stay simple and repeat the main point. You can also choose another trusted person. One difficult conversation does not mean you have to stop looking for support.

Example: “I do not need you to fix everything. I would like you to listen and help me think about what I can do next.”
