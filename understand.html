Understand mental health

Mental health is part of our overall well-being. It affects how we think, feel and behave, and how we cope with stress, build relationships and make decisions. It is not only about mental illness: everyone has mental health, and everyone can look after it.

Why it matters

It shapes learning, concentration, relationships, emotions and daily energy, and it is closely connected with physical health.

It changes over time

Mental health is not fixed. It can be strong in some weeks and strained in others, especially around exams and big changes.

Support works

Talking, routines, rest and professional help can all make a real difference. Early support is easier than waiting.

Stress, anxiety and low mood: how they differ

They can overlap, and everyone has them sometimes. This is general information, not a way to diagnose yourself.

Stress

A reaction to pressure

Feels like: tension, irritability, racing to-do lists.

Usually: linked to a clear cause such as exams, and eases when it passes.

Helps: breaks, planning, sleep, movement, talking.

Anxiety

Ongoing worry or fear

Feels like: a restless mind, "what if" thoughts, a racing heart.

Can: continue even when the stressor is gone.

Helps: slow breathing, routines, talking to a counsellor.

Low mood

Feeling flat or down

Feels like: low energy, loss of interest, feeling empty.

Concerning when: it lasts for weeks and affects daily life.

Helps: reaching out early to someone you trust or a professional.

Real-life situations: what to try

Tap a situation. Each one gives practical ideas and tells you when to talk to someone.

Before an exam

What's happening: Pressure makes your body feel on alert, and it is hard to focus.

Try: Study in 25-minute blocks with 5-minute breaks. Sleep properly the night before. Do a few rounds of slow breathing before you walk in.

Talk to someone if: panic or dread keeps stopping you from studying or attending.

You can't sleep

What's happening: A busy mind and late screens make it hard to switch off.

Try: Keep a regular bedtime, put the phone away 30 minutes before bed, write down worries on paper, then try slow breathing.

Talk to someone if: sleep problems last for weeks.

You had a fight with a friend

What's happening: Conflict hurts, and the mind replays it.

Try: Give it a little time, then say how you felt using "I" sentences. Listen without interrupting.

Talk to someone if: you feel isolated or can't stop thinking about it.

You feel homesick or lonely

What's happening: A new place and routine can feel isolating.

Try: Call home at a fixed time, join one club or study group, and invite one person for tea or a walk.

Talk to someone if: loneliness stays heavy for weeks.

Social media makes you feel behind

What's happening: You compare your everyday life with other people's highlights.

Try: Mute accounts that drain you, set a daily limit, and plan one offline activity you enjoy.

Talk to someone if: comparison is hurting how you see yourself.

You are falling behind in your studies

What's happening: Backlog builds guilt and avoidance.

Try: List what is due, pick the smallest task, do 10 minutes, and ask a teacher for help early.

Talk to someone if: you feel hopeless about catching up.

Family expects too much

What's happening: Family hopes can feel like heavy pressure.

Try: Pick a calm moment and share your honest workload and feelings. Use the starter sentences on the Reflect page.

Talk to someone if: you can't speak to them and the pressure keeps growing. A teacher or counsellor can help.

Common conditions

General information only. Reading about a condition does not mean you have it, and only a qualified professional can diagnose.

Anxiety disorders

Ongoing, hard-to-control worry or fear that affects daily life.

Depression

Persistent low mood or loss of interest lasting for weeks.

OCD

Unwanted repeating thoughts and routines done to ease distress.

PTSD

Lasting distress after a very frightening or traumatic experience.

Eating disorders

Serious difficulties with eating and body image. Professional support matters.

Bipolar disorder

Marked shifts in mood and energy that go beyond ordinary ups and downs.
